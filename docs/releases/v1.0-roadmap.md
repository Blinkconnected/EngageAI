# Future Roadmap

**What comes after v1.0, and why in this order.**

Ordering is by *what unblocks the most*, not by what is most interesting
to build. Anything already recorded as debt or a limitation is
cross-referenced rather than restated.

Estimates are engineering effort, not calendar time.

---

Not a roadmap so much as a list of things that must be true. All three

|---|---|---|

## Next — the pilot period

Work that makes the platform safer to run while it carries its first real
traffic.

### 1. Monitor the monitoring · ~1 day · [TD-2](./v1.0-technical-debt.md#td-2)

A generic `up{job=~".+"} == 0` rule, a blackbox probe through nginx, and
an external dead-man's switch.

**Why first:** every other operational improvement assumes the monitoring
is working. Right now, if Prometheus stops, the platform looks healthy
and silent — which is indistinguishable from healthy and quiet. This is
the last remaining member of the silent-failure family the previous
release existed to eliminate.

### 2. Onboarding wizard gating · ~4 days · [spec](../product/tenant-onboarding-wizard.md)

Derive step completion from server state, require a **verified** channel
connection, add business hours, and replace "Finish" with a go-live
summary.

**Why:** a tenant can currently complete onboarding with no channel
connected — a workspace no customer can reach, reporting itself as set
up. This is the highest-value change to time-to-first-value.

### 3. Navigation grouping · ~1 day · [TD-5](./v1.0-technical-debt.md#td-5)

Six groups instead of seventeen flat items.
[Proposal](../architecture/navigation-information-architecture.md).

**Why here:** cheap, and it compounds with the wizard — both are about an
administrator finding what to do next.

### 4. Delta sync and cursor pagination · ~3 days · [Mobile §8.1–8.2](../api/mobile-integration-guide.md)

`?updated_since=` on conversation lists, and cursor pagination on the two
lists that change while being read.

**Why before a mobile app:** offset paging over a live list can duplicate
or skip rows. Building a client on it means building on a bug.

### 5. Idempotency keys · ~2 days · [Mobile §8.4](../api/mobile-integration-guide.md)

`Idempotency-Key` on creating endpoints.

**Why:** without it, no client can safely retry a `POST` after a timeout
— which on mobile is a routine event, not an exception.

## Then — depends on what the pilot shows

### Learning Platform Phase 3 · [L-7](./v1.0-known-limitations.md)

The intelligence layer currently observes and writes nothing back. Phase 3
opens the gate: memory writes, knowledge suggestions, human review.

**Gated on data, not effort.** Entry criteria: ≥ 1,000 production
observations, ≥ 50 stable clusters, operator review complete, thresholds
finalised, no replay defects. None are met today, and no amount of
engineering advances them — only production traffic does.

**Do not open this gate early.** The whole design of the phase boundary is
that the platform proves its observations are sound before it is allowed
to act on them.

### Native mobile application · [L-8](./v1.0-known-limitations.md)

Feasible once *Next* items 4 and 5 land. Auth, push, device registry and
the agent WebSocket already exist.

Remaining: an APNs sender, documenting the WebSocket contract, and
possibly a read-optimised conversation endpoint. Build order in the
[Mobile Guide §9](../api/mobile-integration-guide.md#9-recommended-build-order).

### Tighter RPO · [L-1](./v1.0-known-limitations.md)

WAL archiving or streaming replication, taking data loss from 24 hours to
minutes.

**Trigger:** revenue-critical volume, or a contractual RPO. Not worth
doing speculatively — it changes how the database is deployed, and that
cost should be paid against a real requirement.

### High availability · [L-2](./v1.0-known-limitations.md)

A warm standby.

**Note the distinction:** availability and capacity have different
answers. A standby fixes availability; horizontal scaling fixes capacity.
Deciding which problem you have first avoids building the wrong one.

## Later — real demand required

Recorded so they are not rediscovered, and deliberately unscheduled.

| Item | Trigger |
|---|---|

| Conversation retention policies | A customer asks for a data-retention commitment ([L-4](./v1.0-known-limitations.md)) |

## Not planned

Stating these prevents re-argument.

- **Exactly-once delivery.** At-least-once with idempotent consumers is
  the correct design. Exactly-once across a broker and a database is a
  distributed-transaction problem that buys less than it costs.
- **A second configuration resolution path.** There is one chain, and a
  build-failing test keeps it that way. Nine once disagreed.
- **Direct broker publishing.** Everything goes through the outbox, or it
  is not transactional and can be lost on rollback.
- **Removing the catch-all routing rule.** It is what makes "a message
  arrives with nowhere to go" impossible.
- **Domain-layer I/O.** The most load-bearing rule in the codebase.

## How to use this

**Before starting anything here**, check the
[V1.0 Baseline](./v1.0-baseline.md) invariants. If the work contradicts
one, that is the conversation to have first.

**Before adding to this list**, ask what it unblocks. Items are ordered
by that, and something that unblocks nothing belongs under *Later* with
its trigger written down.
